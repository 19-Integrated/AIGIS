**Status:** Built — v2.0 (Reconciliation with HOF, HIS-12, and Board Governance) **Type:** Human Authority Instrument **Parent Stack:** HOF (Human Operating Framework) **Version:** 2.0 — Supersedes HAN v1.0

## PREAMBLE

The Human Authority Node (HAN) is the apex constitutional authority for all AI governance within the AIGIS stack. HAN is a single, named human with 5 irreducible properties. No committee, collective, or distributed consensus mechanism may substitute for a single accountability anchor.

HAN is the operational instantiation of HOF's Authority Establishment layer (H1–H4). HAN holds the authority defined in HOF and is governed by HIS-12 (HAN Invariants System). HAN is the human accountability anchor required by ICC-8 I1 (Accountability Invariance).

**The central insight:** Authority without a single named human is not accountability. Committees diffuse responsibility. Distributed consensus obscures liability. HAN is the constitutional answer to the question: *"Who is accountable?"*

## SECTION 1: THE FIVE IRREDUCIBLE PROPERTIES

HAN is defined by five irreducible properties. These are non-negotiable. Any person holding HAN authority must satisfy all five. If any property is absent, the person is not HAN—they are a speed bump.

| Property | Definition | Why It Matters | Enforcement |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **1\. Board Reporting** | HAN reports directly to the CEO or Board Governance Committee, not through CIO, CFO, or COO | Operational pressure cannot override governance authority. HAN cannot be silenced by the C-suite. | Board Governance Cycle Integration; HAN has direct board access; HAN may escalate to Board without operational filter. |
| **2\. Authorization Authority** | HAN can affirmatively authorize AND prohibit Tier 3+ deployments. Not just veto—active authorization. | HAN can say YES and NO. If HAN can only say NO, they are a speed bump, not a gatekeeper. | A-N1 (Trigger Rights) requires HAN authorization for Tier 3+ deployments. CEF Stage 3 (Mandate Close) requires HAN confirmation. |
| **3\. Cross-Functional Mandate** | HAN can direct CIO, CFO, COO, and CXOs on governance matters. They cannot override HAN's governance decisions. | HAN's authority is not advisory. CXOs cannot ignore HAN's governance directives. | HAN's Cross-Functional Mandate is defined in the Constitutional Charter. Control Node Authority Matrix maps AICA-5 nodes to decision-makers—HAN is the apex for cognitive integrity. |
| **4\. Budget Independence** | Governance budget is locked in the Board-approved charter. Cannot be reduced without Board vote. | CFO cannot starve governance. Budget cannot be cut when revenue pressure hits. | Budget Charter protects HAN budget from unilateral CFO reallocation. Budget Deficit State triggers Board review. |
| **5\. No Competing Operational Authority** | HAN's only function is governance. HAN does not hold operational responsibility for IT, Finance, Operations, or business execution. | No conflict of interest between governance authority and operational metrics. HAN is not measured on revenue, cost, or efficiency. | HAN's job description explicitly limits scope to governance. HAN's performance metrics are governance-focused, not operational. |

## SECTION 4: HAN ACCOUNTABILITY

### 4.1 Human Accountability Anchor

HAN is the human accountability anchor for all cognitive integrity and meta-observability functions. All accountability chains for AI reasoning terminate at HAN. Per ICC-8 I1, HAN is operationally reachable—possessing demonstrable capacity to review, override, and bear liability.

### 4.2 Liability

HAN bears liability for:

| Area | Liability |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Failure to detect drift** | HAN bears liability for failure to detect drift that produces material institutional harm. |
| **Certification accuracy** | HAN bears liability for certification of AI systems whose reasoning is subsequently found to be epistemically unsound. |
| **Meta-observability integrity** | HAN bears liability for failure to maintain meta-observability integrity standards. |
| **False or negligent suspension** | HAN bears liability for false or negligent suspension findings. |

HAN does not bear liability for business outcomes of decisions they certified but did not execute or authorize.

### 4.3 Performance Evaluation

HAN is evaluated annually by the Board under the HAN Scorecard System:

| Dimension | Weight | Target |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Decision Quality | 30% | ≥ 9/10 |
| Response Time | 15% | ≤ 14 days average |
| Escalation Accuracy | 20% | ≥ 90% |
| Stakeholder Confidence | 15% | ≥ 8/10 |
| Governance Debt Reduction | 20% | Net debt declining |
| Drift Detection Rate | — | ≥ 95% |
| Certification Accuracy | — | ≥ 90% |
| Meta-Observability Integrity | — | 100% |
| External Legibility | — | "Satisfactory" or higher |

### 4.4 Removal

HAN may be removed by the Board only upon:

| Ground | Definition |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Material Negligence** | HAN fails to perform duties with reasonable care, resulting in material institutional harm. |
| **Misrepresentation** | HAN misrepresents findings, evidence, or reasoning, causing material institutional harm. |
| **Constitutional Violation** | HAN violates ICC-8 invariants (I1–I9), AICA-5 standards, or the Constitutional Charter. |
| **Incapacity** | HAN is physically or mentally unable to perform duties. |
| **Conflict of Interest** | HAN engages in conduct that creates an unresolvable conflict of interest. |
| **Code of Conduct Violation** | HAN materially violates the Governor's Code of Conduct. |

**Removal requires:** Supermajority vote of the Board (two-thirds or more), independent investigation by external review node, and due process.

## SECTION 5: EXECUTIVE OVERRIDE CLAUSE

HAN's Binding Suspension authority could conflict with CEO's operational authority. The override clause resolves this:

| Step | Action | Responsible | Timeline |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1 | HAN triggers suspension, citing specific violations | HAN | Upon detection |
| 2 | Suspension is binding for defined period (72 hours) | HAN | Immediately |
| 3 | CEO may override by issuing formal Executive Override Declaration | CEO | Within 72 hours |
| 4 | Override Declaration acknowledges HAN findings, accepts liability, states business rationale | CEO | — |
| 5 | Upon override, system resumes under CEO's liability | CEO | — |
| 6 | HAN's dissent is escalated to Board for review | HAN | Within 24 hours |

**The Executive Override Declaration must include:**

- Acknowledgment of HAN's suspension findings  
- Statement that CEO accepts full personal and institutional liability  
- Business rationale for overriding  
- Declaration that CEO reviewed HAN's reasoning and disagrees on constitutional, epistemic, or risk grounds

**This resolves the accountability paradox:** HAN holds the authority to suspend. CEO holds the authority to override, accepting liability. Board holds both accountable.

## SECTION 6: HAN CONTINUITY AND SUCCESSION

### 6.1 Deputy Governor

HAN appoints a Deputy Governor with Board confirmation. The Deputy Governor assumes full HAN authority upon HAN unavailability, vacancy, or incapacity.

### 6.2 Third-Tier Succession

A Board Committee is designated to assume HAN authority if both HAN and Deputy Governor are unavailable.

### 6.3 Reduced Operations State

Upon HAN unavailability, vacancy, or incapacity, the organization enters Reduced Operations State:

| Activity | Status |
| :---- | :---- |
| New AI deployments | Suspended |
| Existing deployments | Continue under existing cognitive integrity standards |
| Binding Suspension | Reverts to Board Committee |
| HAN reporting | Suspended |

### 6.4 Minimum Viable Operations State (MVOS)

If full AI workforce is unavailable, HAN operates as sole institutional actor. No new material institutional actions may be initiated. Existing commitments maintained under existing authority.

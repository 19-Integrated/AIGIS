# HOF v2.0 — Human Operating Framework

**Status:** Built — v2.0 (Reconciliation with HAWI, HIS-12, and HAN) **Type:** Human Authority Instrument **Parent Stack:** AIGIS Constitutional Layer **Version:** 2.0 — Supersedes HOF v1.0

---

## PREAMBLE

The Human Operating Framework (HOF) defines the complete authority architecture for human governance within AIGIS. It establishes how human authority is established, executed, and held accountable. HOF answers: *Who holds authority? Under what conditions? With what accountability?*

HOF is the constitutional foundation for all human authority in the AIGIS stack. It is the parent instrument for HAN (Human Authority Node), HIS-12 (HAN Invariants System), and the HAN Scorecard System. It integrates with HAWI (Human-AI Workforce Integration Framework) to bridge human authority and AI workforce governance.

HOF is derived from ICC-8 I1 (Accountability Invariance) and I2 (Authority-Responsibility Separation). No human authority may violate ICC-8 invariants. All human authority must be auditable, attributable, and externally legible.

---

## THE THREE LAYERS OF HOF

HOF is organized into three layers, each containing four invariants (H1–H12):

| Layer | Invariants | Function |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Authority Establishment** | H1–H4 | Defines how authority is created, appointed, and structured |
| **Authority Execution** | H5–H8 | Defines how authority is exercised, delegated, and escalated |
| **Authority Accountability** | H9–H12 | Defines how authority is reviewed, audited, and corrected |

---

## LAYER 1: AUTHORITY ESTABLISHMENT (H1–H4)

### H1 — Human Authority Node (HAN) Appointment Invariance

**Statement:** Authority must be vested in a single, named human (HAN) with 5 irreducible properties. No committee, collective, or distributed consensus mechanism may substitute for a single accountability anchor.

**Violation Condition:** Authority is held by a committee, rotating position, or collective body without a single named anchor.

**Enforcement:** HAN is appointed by the Board. HAC (Human Authority Charter) formalizes the appointment. HAN's 5 properties are verified at charter compilation (CFL-V).

---

### H2 — Board Reporting Invariance

**Statement:** The HAN reports directly to the Board. The HAN is not subordinate to the CEO, C-suite, or any operational executive. The CEO and HAN are constitutional peers.

**Violation Condition:** HAN reporting line passes through CEO, CFO, CIO, or COO.

**Enforcement:** Board Governance Cycle Integration defines HAN reporting cadence and content. HAN has direct board access. HAN may escalate to Board without operational filter.

---

### H3 — Authorization Authority Invariance

**Statement:** The HAN can affirmatively authorize and prohibit AI deployments. The HAN's authority is not limited to veto—it includes active authorization of Tier 3+ deployments.

**Violation Condition:** HAN can only veto, not authorize; or authorization authority is shared with operational executives.

**Enforcement:** HAN's Authorization Authority is defined in the Constitutional Charter. A-N1 (Trigger Rights) requires HAN authorization for Tier 3+ deployments. CEF Stage 3 (Mandate Close) requires HAN confirmation.

---

### H4 — Budget Independence Invariance

**Statement:** The HAN's governance budget is locked in the Board-approved charter. It cannot be reduced without Board approval. The CFO cannot unilaterally reallocate the HAN's budget.

**Violation Condition:** HAN's budget is subject to CFO reallocation or annual cost-cutting cycles without Board approval.

**Enforcement:** Budget Charter protects HAN budget from unilateral CFO reallocation. Budget Deficit State triggers Board review. ERDP includes budget disclosure.

---

## LAYER 2: AUTHORITY EXECUTION (H5–H8)

### H5 — Cross-Functional Mandate Invariance

**Statement:** The HAN can direct the CIO, CFO, COO, and CXOs on governance matters. CXOs cannot override HAN's governance decisions. HAN may direct operational executives on matters of cognitive integrity, meta-observability, and constitutional compliance.

**Violation Condition:** CXOs can override HAN's governance decisions; HAN's mandate is advisory only.

**Enforcement:** HAN's Cross-Functional Mandate is defined in the Constitutional Charter. Control Node Authority Matrix maps AICA-5 nodes to decision-makers—HAN is the apex for cognitive integrity.

---

### H6 — No Competing Operational Authority Invariance

**Statement:** The HAN's only function is governance. The HAN does not hold operational responsibility for IT, Finance, Operations, or business execution. No conflict of interest between governance authority and operational metrics.

**Violation Condition:** HAN also runs IT, Finance, Operations, or any operational function.

**Enforcement:** HAN's job description explicitly limits scope to governance. HAN's performance metrics are governance-focused, not operational. HAN's 5 properties are verified at charter compilation.

---

### H7 — Delegation and Escalation Invariance

**Statement:** The HAN may delegate monitoring and analysis functions to AI agents under AWOF governance. The HAN may not delegate certification, suspension, or constitutional review authority. Escalation paths must be pre-defined and tested.

**Violation Condition:** HAN delegates certification or suspension authority to AI agents; escalation paths are undefined or untested.

**Enforcement:** A-N4 (Escalation Paths) defines routing of decisions and exceptions exceeding current authority levels. ADTEP Escalation Flag forces HAN review when trigger conditions are met. A-N5 (Delegation Boundaries) governs scope, duration, and revocation of delegated authority.

---

### H8 — Override Protocol Invariance

**Statement:** The HAN retains override authority over all AI-performed institutional processes at all times. Emergency suspension capability must be present at all levels. Override mechanisms must be tested by non-technical principals.

**Violation Condition:** Override mechanisms untested; invocation requiring system-level access; override invoked by technical personnel only.

**Enforcement:** A-N3 (Override Protocols) governs conditions and mechanisms for human override. ADTEP Escalation Flag forces HAN review. A-N3 is tested by non-technical principal per governance cycle.

---

## LAYER 3: AUTHORITY ACCOUNTABILITY (H9–H12)

### H9 — Performance Review Invariance

**Statement:** The HAN is evaluated annually by the Board against defined performance metrics. Evaluation integrates HAN self-evaluation, external audit findings, and Board assessment.

**Violation Condition:** No formal performance review; evaluation is ad-hoc or politically motivated.

**Enforcement:** Performance and Accountability Framework defines evaluation metrics, process, and timeline. HAN Scorecard System provides per-role and per-person assessment (9 dimensions \+ composite score). Governor's Reasoning Audit provides independent external validation.

---

### H10 — External Review Invariance

**Statement:** The HAN's reasoning—certifications, suspensions, opinions, and findings—is audited annually by an independent external review node. The review node is structurally independent of the HAN, Board, and C-suite.

**Violation Condition:** No external audit; external audit is controlled by the HAN or Board.

**Enforcement:** Governor's Reasoning Audit is conducted by an independent external review node. Audit findings are published in Governor's Public Register. CEN-5 (Independence) requires structural separation.

---

### H11 — Succession and Continuity Invariance

**Statement:** Succession of accountability must be defined for all critical human authority roles. Institutional function must persist through HAN unavailability, vacancy, or incapacity.

**Violation Condition:** No defined succession; institution ceases to function on HAN unavailability.

**Enforcement:** Deputy Governor and Third-Tier Succession ensure continuity. MVOS (Minimum Viable Operations State) defines HAN-only operations when AI workforce is unavailable. Reduced Operations State defined in Continuity and Succession Charter.

---

### H12 — Removal and Due Process Invariance

**Statement:** The HAN may be removed only by Board supermajority vote, after independent investigation and due process. Removal is not arbitrary or politically motivated.

**Violation Condition:** HAN removed by simple majority; removal without investigation; removal for political reasons.

**Enforcement:** Removal requires supermajority vote (two-thirds or more). Independent investigation by external review node precedes removal. Grounds for removal are defined (material negligence, misrepresentation, constitutional violation, incapacity, conflict of interest).

## HAN SCORECARD SYSTEM

The HAN Scorecard System provides per-role and per-person assessment across 9 dimensions, producing a composite score.

| Dimension | Weight | Assessment Criteria |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Decision Quality** | 30% | Decisions made, appeal rate, quality assessment, confidence score |
| **Response Time** | 15% | Average decision latency, on-time rate, bottleneck assessment |
| **Escalation Accuracy** | 20% | Escalations handled, correct escalation decision rate, over/under-escalation rate |
| **Stakeholder Confidence** | 15% | CIO trust, CFO trust, COO trust, Board confidence, composite |
| **Governance Debt Reduction** | 20% | Debt introduced, debt cleared, net debt trend, projection |
| **Detectability** | — | Drift detection rate, false positive rate, false negative rate |
| **Certification Accuracy** | — | Certification accuracy, certification turnaround time |
| **Observability Integrity** | — | Observability coverage, meta-observability integrity |
| **External Legibility** | — | External audit rating, regulatory satisfaction, contestation rate |

**Composite Score:** Weighted sum of all dimensions. Target: 8.5+ / 10.0
